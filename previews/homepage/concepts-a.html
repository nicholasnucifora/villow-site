Design concepts & open questions

Ideas beyond Villow.

Designs for better video and social platforms, and the questions they raise. Open a topic to explore.

These are concepts and discussions, separate from Villow’s available features.

01Context that reaches youA useful note, even after you have watched.Design concept

A correction should reach you, too.

A note could add context beside a video. If it arrives later, a persistent notice could reach people who already watched.

What needs working out?

People with different viewpoints would need to agree that a note is helpful. Moderation, who can contribute, and whether notices actually reach people would need testing.

Platform participation could help this work at scale. This remains a proposal.

02A next step beyond the videoOne focused action from the creator.Design concept

A good video. A useful next step.

A creator could put one focused action beside a video: join a local group, attend an event or help a cause.

How could it reach viewers?

Native integration would need YouTube’s support. An independent service for verified creator submissions is another possible path, still an early idea.

The aim is one relevant next step, with links the creator trusts. Neither route is available through this concept today.

03Recommendations from peopleDiscovery shaped by what people found worthwhile.Exploring

A recommendation, with a reason.

What if people could recommend a video because it was worth their time, and say why? A shared discovery service could bring those picks to different Villows.

The aim is to surface worthwhile videos, with understandable reasons for why they reached you. Popularity alone would not establish quality.

The difficult part

Bots and coordinated voting could manipulate recommendations too. Any experiment would need ways to challenge abuse, make ranking understandable and give people control over discovery.

04Where could AI help?Six possibilities, with a purpose and a tradeoff for each.Early ideas

Useful help should earn its place.

Villow works without generative AI. Its current suggestions use transparent rules based on your ratings, skips and usage. These six possibilities are early ideas, with no imminent release plans. Each would need to show a useful benefit over simpler tools.

Find your place in a video

Labelled timestamps could help you find a useful moment or return to something you want to revisit.

Wrong labels or missing visual context could misrepresent a video.

Our take. Start with creator-provided chapters. Explore generated ones only when source access is permitted and timestamps can be checked.

Ask a question about a video

Ask a focused question or revisit an argument without searching the whole video.

A fluent answer can still be wrong, and transcripts may be incomplete or unavailable.

Our take. Any experiment should cite the source, separate added context and make it easy to return to the video.

Think through a comment

Explore unfamiliar wording or consider a clarifying question before replying.

A short comment rarely gives enough context to settle its author’s intent or a claim’s truth.

Our take. Keep the original visible and treat generated interpretations as possibilities, never credibility scores.

Describe your topic preferences

Describe what you want to find, such as more archaeology and fewer battle histories.

Ambiguous preferences could silently narrow what reaches you.

Our take. Only explore this with an inspectable interpretation, a preview, and ordinary controls to change or undo it.

Refine recommendations in your words

Explain what you want from discovery and explore material that simple filters might miss.

Opaque AI ranking could recreate the loss of control Villow is trying to address. It would not import YouTube’s personalized feed.

Our take. Defer it unless it shows a useful benefit over simpler, explained choices.

Consider what to do next

Talk through what you want to do next, including rest or doing nothing.

Personal plans can be sensitive, and advice could add pressure or become another endless conversation.

Our take. Leave decisions and changes with you. A simple list may already be enough.

Before any of these became a feature

Before building any of these, we would need clear choices about privacy, creator permissions, reliability, resource use and cost. People should know what data is sent, be able to turn the feature off, and stay in control of what changes.

05Your choices across the webA picture of your time that belongs to you.A longer view

See where your attention goes.

A user-owned picture of time across platforms could help people decide where attention goes, with limits and reflection that follow their choices.

Today, Villow covers its own usage and YouTube in connected extension browsers. Wider coverage would need new integrations, clear consent and a way to keep that picture under the person’s control.

06Comments, bots and trustAn open problem that goes well beyond Villow.Open question

Trust matters more than volume.

Comments help us make sense of what we watch. Fake or coordinated engagement can make manufactured agreement look real, leaving people unsure who or what to trust.

A smaller conversation worth trusting would be more valuable than a flood of uncertain accounts. But calling someone a bot can silence a real person, too.

No simple test for trust

Public platforms need ways to limit automated manipulation, protect real people from false accusations and let them challenge mistakes. Knowing a person is human would not make every claim they post true.

We would rather have ten comments people can trust than a thousand they can’t.

Start with people you know.

This is one reason Villow makes space for people you know, and conversation beyond the screen.

Villow cannot verify the wider internet or solve comment botting. This belongs here as an important open problem.

YouTube prohibits artificial comments and other fake engagement. Read its fake engagement policy.
