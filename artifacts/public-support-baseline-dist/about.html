About

A working example of a web built for people.

Fix the Web campaigns for people's control and wellbeing online. Villow turns that ambition into a product you can use, question and improve. It demonstrates possible controls; it doesn't yet prove their effects.

Visit Fix the Web →

Who built it

Villow was designed and built by Nicholas Nucifora, founder of Fix the Web.

Nicholas intends to continue developing and maintaining Villow, and to use working tools like it to help change how the web works for people.

Why “Villow”?

Video plus pillow, somewhere comfortable to watch. Like willow and meadow, the name also leaves room for life outside the feed. Watching for entertainment, comfort or rest counts too.

Help Villow reach people.

A useful prototype only has a chance to change things if people actually use it. You can help in several ways:

  • Use Villow: create your own or join a friend's, and see what fits your day.
  • Invite friends: give people a trusted place to try it and something to watch together.
  • Spread the word: share it with creators, communities and people with reach. Describe what you found useful rather than promising an outcome.
  • Give feedback: optional check-ins and the app's Feedback page help improve the design.
  • Contribute: code, bug reports, accessibility feedback and tested owner guidance.
  • Support the wider movement: Fix the Web campaigns for these kinds of choices across the internet.

What we're learning

Villow is a substantial working prototype. We want to learn whether finite feeds, optional reflection, explained suggestions and social support help people spend time in ways they value. These are design intentions, not established wellbeing or polarization results.

Private viewing measurements stay in the Villow you use. Only feedback you choose to submit goes to Fix the Web. Voluntary feedback can strengthen the evidence, but self-reported surveys have limits. Findings will need context about who participated and how they were measured.
