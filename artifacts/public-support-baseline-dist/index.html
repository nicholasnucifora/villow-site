What's inside

More say over your day.

  • Channels, on your terms

    Keep a channel's tutorials, exclude a series you skip, choose a duration range, or catch up with it weekly. A few choices shape the feed around you.

  • Your limits follow you

    Your limits follow you across Villow and connected browsers. Count Villow usage and YouTube pages in browsers with the linked extension; native YouTube apps and unconnected browsers are outside that coverage.

  • Stop now. Pick up where you left off.

    Saved progress gives a video somewhere to wait. Optional Watch Later size limits, expiry and pinning help keep the library manageable.

  • Suggestions that show their reasons

    Your ratings, skips and usage can point to an adjustment. Villow waits for a sufficient pattern, explains it, and leaves the decision to you.

  • Built for friends, not followers

    Recommend a video, share what you choose, or watch together in a private room with synchronized playback, a shared queue and chat.

  • Something else you'd enjoy

    Bring in your own activities from Google Tasks or Todoist. At a natural stopping point, they're an invitation to do what matters to you, including rest.

An algorithm that works for you

Not just how long. Whether it was worth it.

Time alone can't tell you whether a video was worthwhile. Optional reflections add your perspective. When enough of your ratings and usage show a pattern, Villow suggests a change, such as checking a channel less often, and shows why. You choose whether to act. It never suggests raising a limit.

Boundaries you choose

A good place to stop.

Daily and per-channel limits, schedules, bedtime and enforced breaks help you keep the boundaries you chose. A finish-current-video allowance is available for some limits when the app offers it. Streaks count days without raising or extending limits, rather than simply days below a time threshold.

Your own private Villow. Your people.

One person sets up a Villow on hosting and database accounts they control, then friends sign in. You can run your own or join one run by someone you trust. The owner controls the database; your member sharing choices decide what other members see through the app.

YouTube could offer these controls to everyone.

Villow demonstrates that these controls are possible. YouTube could offer them directly to everyone. Fix the Web is building the case through working tools and voluntary feedback. Villow is a prototype we can learn from; better outcomes need evidence, and private viewing data isn't automatically sent to the movement.
