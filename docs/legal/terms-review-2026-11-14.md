## Release prerequisites

1. **Deployment-specific policy facts.** Every independent operator must identify themselves, provide a working contact and adopted/applicable terms and privacy, and state their providers/regions, retention, backup and request arrangements. A link to the project's page does not make Nicholas the operator of every installation. Check the actual receiving mailbox provider, provider locations/logs/backups and deployed cleanup schedule. Apply a justified accounting/mail retention practice before promising a fixed expiry.
2. **YouTube lifecycle verification.** The controlling Developer Policies distinguish in-client revocation (immediate token revocation; Authorized Data deletion within seven days), external Google revocation (periodic validity checks; related API-data deletion within 30 days), user deletion/account deletion (seven days), and other API-cache refresh/deletion (generally 30 days, with specified exceptions). Verify inactive accounts, scheduled retries, backups and manual request handling on the eventual deployment. The explanatory guide's looser language is not a reason to ignore the controlling policy.
3. **Focused Australian legal review.** Assess Privacy Act coverage rather than assuming a sole-trader/small-business exemption. The statutory exceptions and actual wellbeing representations/data matter. Assess eSafety social-media classification against the app's personalised content, feedback/view indicators, friends, recommendations and chat. Finite feeds, private hosting, small size and open-source licensing do not themselves settle that test. The 16+ product rule and self-attestation do not establish required age assurance if the service is covered. Review international obligations where the service's activities bring them into scope.
4. **Payment/store release checks.** Verify actual Stripe sharing questions, cancellation timing and earlier supporter offers in a controlled workflow. Align shipped Chrome/Edge/Firefox data disclosures, licences and consent behavior with the reviewed revision. Recheck Mozilla's agreement for releases on/after 1 December 2026. These are not authorised live changes now.
5. **Final version and publication.** Review the legal wording/operator model, select an effective date at release, obtain explicit owner publication instruction, publish the approved documents first, verify URLs/versions, then enable the appropriate product configuration. Explain material changes and obtain required renewed agreement. Do not accept this draft or record production receipts against it.

The privacy work is also not a complete YouTube API-client compliance audit. In particular, the connected extension supplies metadata read from YouTube pages and changes those pages' controls. Review how Developer Policies III.E.6 (scraping/obtaining scraped data) and III.F.1 (changes to YouTube interfaces) apply to that architecture, and obtain any necessary platform approval. Do not describe store acceptance, MIT licensing or these policies as approval from YouTube. This is a concrete platform review item; it does not authorise removing or redesigning those features during the local policy task.
